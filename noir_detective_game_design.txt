==============================================================================
NOIR DETECTIVE: A CRITICAL THINKING MYSTERY GAME
Design and Flow Document (working title)
==============================================================================

Status: Draft v1, based on decisions made in planning conversation
Platform: Web browsers
Hosting / tech stack: Intentionally left open for now


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. OVERVIEW
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A browser-based detective game that builds critical thinking and reading
comprehension. Players take on short mystery cases (3-5 minutes each) drawn
from public-domain detective fiction and real, non-violent solved cases.
They examine evidence, interview suspects, catch contradictions, connect
clues, and make a final accusation backed by cited evidence. Scores feed a
global, all-time leaderboard.

Design pillars
  1. Reasoning over guessing: points reward HOW you solved it, not only
     whether you named the right suspect.
  2. Real roots: every case is anchored in a real story or real solved case.
     The solution is never invented.
  3. Short and replayable: one case fits in a coffee break.
  4. Welcoming to all ages: selectable reading level, non-graphic content,
     age-aware privacy.
  5. Fair competition: the leaderboard accounts for difficulty and reading
     level, and scoring is verified server-side.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. AUDIENCE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

All ages. Three reading levels adapt the same case:
  - Kids:     short sentences, simple vocabulary, gentler framing
  - General:  everyday adult reading level
  - Advanced: richer vocabulary, subtler clues, more ambiguity

Reading level is chosen by the player before each case and can be changed
in settings.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. CORE GAME LOOP
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Pick a case --> Choose reading level --> Briefing --> Investigate
  (evidence, interviews, contradictions, deduction board) --> Accuse with
  cited evidence --> Reveal and score --> Real-case debrief --> Leaderboard
  --> Pick the next case

Target length per case: 3-5 minutes.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. CASE LIBRARY AND CONTENT RULES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 Launch library
  - 10 cases at launch (proof of concept)
  - Players can pick any case from the library at any time
  - Cases are grouped by theme (see 4.3)

4.2 Allowed sources
  - Public-domain detective fiction (for example Sherlock Holmes, Poe,
    Chesterton)
  - Historical solved cases based on public records
  - Non-violent real cases (fraud, theft, art heists, espionage)

4.3 Themes
  Players choose a theme to filter the library. Suggested starting themes:
    - Classic Detective Fiction
    - Art and Heists
    - Fraud and Forgery
    - Espionage and Secrets
    - Historical Puzzles
  (Final theme list to be set once the 10 launch cases are chosen.)

4.4 Content safety rules
  - No graphic or violent real cases. Real cases involving violence against
    real victims are excluded.
  - Fiction may be mysterious or tense but stays non-graphic at every
    reading level.
  - Every case carries a source credit (story, author, or historical record).

4.5 AI adaptation rules
  The AI adapts each case live when a player starts it. It MAY:
    - Rewrite narration and clues at the chosen reading level
    - Add fictional suspects, witnesses and red herrings
    - Generate suspect dialogue consistent with the case facts
  It MUST NOT:
    - Change the real solution or the key real facts of the case
    - Introduce details that make the solution ambiguous or unsolvable
    - Add graphic or inappropriate content

4.6 Case "answer key" (per case, stored server-side)
  Each case has a verified master record containing:
    - The true culprit / solution
    - The set of essential clues that prove the solution
    - Known contradictions that must be catchable
    - Facts the AI may not alter
  The AI-adapted version is validated against this record before play
  (see section 11, Validation).


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. PLAYER ACTIONS (GAMEPLAY MECHANICS)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 Examine Evidence
  - The player inspects clues (documents, objects, locations, notes).
  - Each clue goes into the Evidence Locker with a short description.
  - Some clues are essential, some are red herrings.

5.2 Interview Suspects
  - Dialogue-choice interviews. Each suspect has a statement and
    can answer several questions.
  - Choices reveal new information, and some may only be asked once.
  - Statements are logged in a Case Notebook for later comparison.

5.3 Spot Contradictions
  - The player can flag two items (statement vs. statement, or statement
    vs. evidence) as contradictory.
  - Correct flags reward points and often unlock new leads.
  - Incorrect flags cost a small penalty, which discourages random guessing.

5.4 Deduction Board
  - A visual board where the player links clues, statements and suspects
    ("this alibi conflicts with that receipt").
  - Links can be labeled (supports / contradicts / suggests motive).
  - The board is the player's reasoning map and feeds the reasoning score.

5.5 Final Accusation
  - The player names the culprit and selects the specific evidence that
    supports the accusation (cited evidence).
  - The accusation is checked against the case's answer key.
  - Right suspect with weak evidence scores lower than right suspect with
    strong evidence.

5.6 Hints
  - Hints are always available and free to request, but each one costs
    points.
  - Hint tiers escalate: a nudge, then a direction, then a strong pointer.
  - Each tier costs more than the last.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. SCORING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

7.1 Score components
  1. Correct solution:    largest component. Right culprit named.
  2. Reasoning quality:   evidence cited correctly in the accusation,
                          contradictions correctly flagged, useful links on
                          the deduction board.
  3. Speed:               time bonus. Capped, so reading carefully is never
                          punished too harshly.
  4. Hints:               deduction per hint, escalating by hint tier.
  5. Penalties:           incorrect contradiction flags, wrong accusation.

7.2 Normalization
  Raw scores are adjusted so different cases, reading levels and difficulty
  levels are comparable:
    - Each case has a difficulty rating (Easy / Medium / Hard)
    - Each reading level has a multiplier
    - Final leaderboard score = raw score x difficulty multiplier x
      reading-level multiplier
  The scoring weights and multipliers need playtesting. The structure above
  is the proposal and the numbers are not final.

7.3 Rule for reading levels
  Multipliers should reward challenge fairly without making the Kids level
  feel like a lesser tier. Kids-level players should still be able to rank
  well among their peers (see 8.3).


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. LEADERBOARD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

8.1 Type
  Global, all-time.

8.2 What is ranked
  - Overall leaderboard: total normalized score across all cases
  - Per-case view: best normalized score for each case

8.3 Fairness options to consider
  - Filter by reading level so players can compare against peers
  - Optional filters: theme, difficulty

8.4 Display
  Rank, nickname, total score, cases solved, top badge. Nicknames only are
  shown publicly. Emails are never displayed.

8.5 Integrity
  See section 10 (Anti-Cheat).


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. ACCOUNTS, PRIVACY AND SAFETY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

9.1 Sign-in options
  - Nickname-only play
  - Email sign-in (optional)

9.2 Age check
  - Age is requested at sign-up and the experience adjusts accordingly.
  - Under 13: nickname only, no email or other personal data collected, no
    free-text input shared publicly. (Designed to align with children's
    privacy rules such as COPPA. Legal review is recommended before launch.)
  - 13 and over: email sign-in available.

9.3 Nickname moderation
  - Profanity and abuse filter on nicknames
  - Report / remove mechanism for inappropriate names
  - Players under 13 pick from approved name parts instead of typing free
    text (suggested approach, e.g. "Clever" + "Fox")

9.4 Data minimization
  Collect only what is needed: nickname, optional email, age bracket, scores.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. ANTI-CHEAT AND SCORE INTEGRITY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Principle: the browser never decides the result.

  - The answer key and true solution stay on the server and are never sent
    to the player's browser.
  - Accusations are checked server-side.
  - The server measures time itself and calculates the score from logged
    player actions. The browser sends actions, not scores.
  - Rate limiting on game starts, hint requests and score submissions.
  - Basic anomaly flags (impossibly fast solves, repeated perfect runs on
    new cases).
  - Nickname filtering (see 9.3).


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. AI CASE ADAPTATION PIPELINE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Per game start:

  1. Player selects a case and a reading level.
  2. Server loads the case's verified master record (facts, solution,
     essential clues, contradictions, source).
  3. The AI generates the playable version: narration, clue text,
     fictional suspects and red herrings, and interview dialogue, all at the
     chosen reading level.
  4. Validation before play:
       - Real solution unchanged
       - All essential clues present
       - Required contradictions still discoverable
       - Fictional additions do not create a second plausible culprit
       - Content check against the safety rules (section 4.4)
  5. If validation fails: regenerate, or fall back to a pre-approved
     version of the case so the player is never left stuck.
  6. Player plays the validated version. The server holds the answer key.

Recommended safety net: keep a reviewed fallback version of each of the
10 launch cases.

Cost control (open item): a free daily play limit is the suggested
starting point. Final approach to be decided.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. FEEDBACK AND PROGRESSION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

12.1 After each case
  - Total score with a clear breakdown
  - Reasoning strengths, for example:
        Evidence Use ........ how well cited evidence supported the claim
        Contradiction Spotting ... correct vs. incorrect flags
        Questioning ......... useful interview choices made
        Connecting the Dots ... quality of deduction board links
  - A short, encouraging tip on the weakest area
  - The Real-Case Debrief: what really happened, with source credit

12.2 Progress over time
  - Skill meters for each reasoning strength
  - Badges (examples: "Sharp Eye" for catching all contradictions,
    "No Hints Needed", "Case Closed in Record Time")
  - Daily play streaks
  - History of completed cases and best scores

12.3 Purpose
  Players should see their critical thinking improving, not just their rank.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
13. LOOK, FEEL AND AUDIO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Visual style: vintage noir. Think black-and-white with warm amber accents,
typewriter and case-file typography, paper textures, case folders, corkboard
for the deduction board.

Audio: noir-style ambient music and sound effects (pages turning, stamps,
typewriter clicks). Include a simple mute toggle.

Note: Accessibility features (screen reader support, dyslexia-friendly
font, colorblind-safe palette) were not selected, but are recommended for an
all-ages audience. Worth revisiting.
